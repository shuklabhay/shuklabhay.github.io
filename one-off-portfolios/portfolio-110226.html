Designing better neural recording devices now

Even solving small problems with hardware here has extreme leverage, and could make the same results possible with OOMs less data. Using ideas from my work, my time in SF's neurotechnology scene, and random papers, I'm designing my own neural recording devices, from my dorm room desk:)

To me, the biggest bottleneck to non-invasive BCI is resolution. Data needed ∝ (measurement noise)², but all our ways to noninvasively record data don't go deep enough or are annoying for other reasons (the brain is ~15 cm in diameter; EEG goes ~1 cm deep, fNIRS blurs over ~1–3 cm with an extreme time delay, the best ultrasound gets ~8–9 cm but injects microbubbles into your bloodstream and still suffers from extreme lag).

AI + bio

Gevaert Lab @ Stanford – Student Researcher2024-2025

During my junior and senior years of HS, I worked at a Stanford lab training large neural networks on medical imagery (an extremely data-starved regime, since scans are expensive and private).

The main project I worked on created enormous amounts of high-quality synthetic medical image data from a single 2D/3D lung scan: using differential equations and ViTs, we segment a scan, build a multi-scale digital model of the patient’s lungs, then simulate a full breathing cycle and render scans at every phase.

I also worked on some SSL projects to better understand breast tissue variation among populations.

Multimodal lesion classification 2025For my senior capstone, with support from OpenAI (Codex for OSS grant) and Modal (compute credits), I trained <500M models to classify skin diseases from images and text. To do this, I built massive synthetic data pipelines (it's surprisingly hard to engineer diversity!) and wrote kernels for MLX inference.
